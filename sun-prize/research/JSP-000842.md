## Proof accessibility

Good to moderate. Bondy's paper is 12 pages and its abstract explicitly states an Erdős conjecture: every graph of order `n` and size at least `(n² - 5n + 14)/2` has a cycle of length `n - 1`. It also proves degree-based lower bounds for circumference.

## Proof structure profile

Classical finite extremal graph theory: longest path/cycle arguments, degree sums and closure-style transformations. There is no analytic or probabilistic layer.

## Likely Lean / Mathlib prerequisites

- `Mathlib.Combinatorics.SimpleGraph.Hamiltonian`
- walks, paths and cycles
- finite vertex/edge cardinalities
- degrees and degree sums
- induced/subgraphs and longest-path maximality arguments

## Formalization bottlenecks

- Mathlib has Hamiltonian-cycle infrastructure, but the theorem needs a cycle missing exactly one vertex rather than a fully Hamiltonian cycle.
- “Choose a longest cycle/path” arguments require finite maximization and careful support-cardinality lemmas.
- Translating old graph-theory notation into current `SimpleGraph` APIs may dominate early work.
