## Proof accessibility

Good. Later literature describes the result as the cycle-plus-triangles theorem: an edge-disjoint union of a Hamiltonian cycle and vertex-disjoint triangles is 3-colourable; Fleischner–Stiebitz in fact prove a stronger list-colouring statement. Modern descriptions explicitly say their proof uses the Alon–Tarsi / Combinatorial Nullstellensatz method.

## Likely proof structure

1. Encode a cycle-plus-triangles graph and its Hamiltonian cycle / triangle decomposition.
2. Translate a proper list colouring into a polynomial nonvanishing problem.
3. Identify the relevant monomial/coefficient via an orientation/parity count.
4. Apply the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz to obtain a colouring from 3-element lists.
5. Specialize list-colourability to ordinary 3-colourability.

The 1992 paper must be read line-by-line before fixing the exact polynomial statement.

## Formalization bottlenecks

- The decomposition is edge-based while coloring is vertex-based; representing the assumptions elegantly matters.
- The algebraic coefficient calculation may be the true proof core and can be much more verbose in Lean than on paper.
- The official JSP statement must be matched to the exact theorem variant proved in 1992 (ordinary vs list coloring, graph simplicity, multiplicity conventions).
